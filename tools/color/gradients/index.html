Why gradients go muddy: the visual version

The widgets from the article Why gradients go muddy, in one place, for when you would rather play than read. Under each heading is a short note on what that widget illustrates. The last one lets you pick any two colors.

One blend, five spaces

Blue to yellow, blended in five color spaces. The formula is always the same straight line; only the numbers it is drawn in change. The middle of the bar goes from dead gray to mauve to teal depending on the space.

The path through the gray point

The same five blends drawn as paths on the a*/b* plane of Lab. The gray cross in the middle is where color disappears: some paths go straight through it, others pass it at a distance.

#0000FF to #FFFF00, seen in Lab

  • Encoded sRGB#808080 · L* 54 · C 0
  • Linear light#BCBCBC · L* 76 · C 0
  • HSL#00FF80 · L* 88 · C 90
  • Lab#CA8AAA · L* 65 · C 30
  • Oklab#6CABC7 · L* 67 · C 24
50%

Each line is one gradient space, drawn on the a*/b* plane (looking down the lightness axis). The gray cross is where color disappears. The dots are the colors at the chosen position.
